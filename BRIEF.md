# Fix the Island

An original, landscape browser restoration adventure. Walk a real three-dimensional island, discover broken infrastructure, solve hands-on repair activities, and watch routes and the environment change. This is a game first, not a dashboard, circuit editor, trading contest, or spaceship reskin.

Loop: explore -> approach a site -> inspect the fault -> interact with a physical repair puzzle -> test -> restore -> new path/objective and visible world change. Target a complete 15–25 minute island journey with at least six distinct repair sites across coast, woodland, and upland, multiple puzzle families, progression, persistent local save, a clear ending and replay. Mistakes provide actionable feedback and retry without losing the expedition. No arbitrary survival timer. Repairs must not be one-click checklists.

Camera: comfortable high-angle, near-orthographic third-person tracking, original authored low-poly 3D environment with rich layered terrain, paths, shoreline, cliffs, foliage, structures and human-scale equipment. No map background image. Use a real collision/traversal model and camera-relative controls. Warm coastal/woodland daylight; cool atmospheric upland provides the dialogue reference's lighting slot. Final camera proportions lock at Stage B. Hero is an original clothed island restorer, Blender-authored skinned GLB, with grounded alternating-leg locomotion and idle/repair states, never a hopping capsule or assembled primitive mascot.

Platform/stack: Three.js + TypeScript in the browser, landscape desktop and phone, keyboard/WASD/arrows + touch joystick/interact, sensible portrait guidance, pause/mute/settings, readable minimap and compact contextual game HUD. Real shadows, surface roughness/normal variation, contact occlusion, fog/grade, low-cost bloom where appropriate, instanced vegetation and bounded pixel ratio. Keep graphics behind a renderer contract; gameplay state must not depend on Three.js meshes.

GenLayer boundary (updated by explicit human direction during Stage A): browser runs immediate movement, collision and hands-on construction. Each repair produces a bounded typed artifact (parts, configuration, connections and a short natural-language rationale), not a completed flag. An intelligent Python contract independently checks that construct against its own immutable site requirements: deterministic safety/connectivity/budget checks first, then independent validators judge contextual engineering/ecological reasoning and agree on structured decision flags, not identical prose. In live mode, the game restores a site only after finalized successful execution and a matching caller/site/nonce/digest-bound approved contract record. Pending review permits continued exploration; dependent routes remain locked until approval. GenLayer validates the submitted design, NOT physical play or a client score. No NFT or payout. An explicitly separate local practice mode can restore with local deterministic checks; label it honestly and never promote local progress into chain approval. SDK integration exposes public configuration, explicit player wallet approval, submission and execution/finality/error/rejection/recovery states. Unconfigured play is visibly local; never fabricate chain or AI success. No secrets or custodial signer. No deployment, public publishing, wallet creation/unlock, paid API calls or spending without separate human authorization.

World progression (explicit human direction): the island begins storm-damaged, chaotic and subdued. Accepted repairs visibly quiet malfunctioning equipment, bring back water/power/light, reopen routes, clear damage and progressively restore vegetation, colour and environmental calm. This gradual transformation is part of the core loop, not only a final percentage badge.

Pending repair presentation (explicit human direction during Stage A): while a submitted design is under review, a small visible crew prepares the site with inspection/hammering and scaffolding so the player can leave and explore. This is an honest pending state, not fake completion or a predicted chain timer. Only finalized successful approval triggers the short completion glow, scaffold removal, machinery activation and terrain recovery. Rejection pauses the crew and marks the saved design for revision; timeout preserves a recoverable pending job. Reuse the final skinned character family for workers rather than introduce primitive placeholder people.

Education: systems thinking, diagnosing faults, resource allocation and explaining environmental trade-offs, not SQL. Maintain repair agency: explanations should reflect the actual solved puzzle and changed world.
